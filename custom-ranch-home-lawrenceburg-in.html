A ranch built for a full house

This Lawrenceburg home was designed for a household that needs room to spread out. It is a ranch with five bedrooms, three on the main level and two on the finished lower level, in 2,966 finished square feet. That is enough space for a family, guests and a home office without any room feeling oversized.

The style is modern and transitional: clean lines, flat-panel cabinetry, black accents against white walls and a neutral wood-look floor that runs through the main level. Construction took about seven months in 2024, and this page walks through the house room by room.

A modern entry

The entry sets the tone. A black front door with three frosted glass panels and matching sidelights lets daylight into the foyer while keeping the front of the house private. At the garage, a black door with a row of horizontal windows sits against brick and a gray stone base, repeating the dark accent color from the front door.

Inside the door, the foyer opens to the great room on one side and, on the other, to a staircase with a glass railing that leads to the lower level.

A great room organized around the fireplace

The great room is the center of the main level. A linear fireplace in a white surround stands on a partial wall that separates the room from the entry and the kitchen without closing either off, so you can see from one end of the house to the other. Large windows on the back wall fill the room with light, and a ceiling fan keeps the air moving in summer.

Because the plan is open, furniture can be arranged in several ways, and the kitchen, dining area and living space all read as one connected room.

A kitchen of clean lines and hidden storage

The kitchen is a bright, uncluttered space. White flat-panel cabinets run to the ceiling, the countertops are a white stone-look surface with soft veining, and a black range with a stainless hood gives the room a strong focal point against all that white. A sliding glass door beside the kitchen opens to the back deck, so cooking and outdoor dining are only a step apart.

Behind the clean fronts, the storage works hard. Tall pantry cabinets hold pull-out wood drawers and adjustable shelves, and flush panel doors hide still more storage. It is the kind of kitchen that stays calm even when it is in use.

Bedrooms and baths with a hotel feel

With five bedrooms, bathrooms matter, and the three full baths each have their own character. The primary bath has two vanities in a soft gray finish with round, backlit mirrors above them, plus a glass-enclosed shower with tall vertical tile and a built-in niche. A separate walk-in closet offers shelving along multiple walls.

Another bath breaks from the gray with a deep navy vanity and an oval backlit mirror, which gives the room personality without a lot of added expense. Frameless glass, simple chrome fixtures and large-format tile keep every bath bright and easy to clean.

A lower level with its own living space

A staircase with a glass rail and a continuous handrail leads down to the finished lower level, which adds 882 square feet. The centerpiece is a family room with a second linear fireplace, this one framed in a dark gray surround, with a ceiling fan overhead and small windows that bring in daylight.

A wet bar sits just off the room, with a sink and cabinetry for drinks and snacks, which makes the lower level a natural place for movie nights and game days. Two of the home's five bedrooms are on this level, giving guests or older kids some privacy from the rest of the house.

Everyday details

The laundry room has its own cabinets and counter, plus a sliding barn-style door that saves the space a door would swing into. These smaller rooms are where a home tends to succeed or fail day to day, and they were planned with as much care as the great room.
