Contemporary Lake House Renovation

A contemporary lake house renovation in Hidden Valley Lake, Indiana, with a sunken great room, a large opening glass wall and a lake-facing deck.

38 photos

Opening a lake house to the water

This Hidden Valley Lake renovation is a contemporary lake home built around the view. The project reworked the primary suite, the living and dining areas, the foyer, the laundry, a powder room and the deck, along with work upstairs. The guiding idea was simple: make the lake the main feature of every room that faces it.

The renovation took about five months, with the final walkthrough and details in early fall. Here is a look at how it came together.

A sunken great room and a wall of glass

The centerpiece is the great room. It sits a few steps down from the surrounding spaces, which gives it a feeling of its own, and its entire lake-facing wall is glass. The sliding panels open to the deck, and when they do, the room and the outdoors become one space.

A smooth, neutral floor in the sunken room lets the view be the color, and the raised, warm wood floors around it provide a clear change in feeling from one level to the next.

A deck made for the view

Beyond the glass, an expansive deck stretches across the lake side of the house, with a black metal railing that keeps the view open. It connects directly to the great room, so the two spaces work together when you host.

Wood details that warm the modern finishes

Contemporary can feel cold, so wood does a lot of work in this house. Vertical wood slats form the railing along the upper hallway and the open stairs, letting light pass through while still giving a sense of enclosure. The stair treads float, which adds to the airy feel, and light oak floors run through the main living spaces.

The warm tones repeat in the natural-wood cabinetry used for the baths and built-ins.

A pop of color in the details

Amid the white walls and natural wood, a soft seafoam green appears in cabinetry. A floating vanity in the powder room is finished in the color, and a built-in wall of cabinets with open shelving uses it too. It is a quiet nod to the water, and it keeps the interior from feeling too plain.

A primary suite with a view of its own

The primary suite is arranged to take in the lake. A bay of black-framed windows frames the water from the bedroom, and the light oak floor reflects the daylight. The bath continues the modern look with a double vanity in natural wood, a white stone-look top, wall-mounted black faucets and a large walk-in shower finished in big tile with a niche and handheld sprayer.

A walk-in closet with shelving and hanging rods sits close by, and tall built-in storage keeps the suite tidy.

A renovation designed around how you live

The result is a lake house that feels connected to its setting, with clear sightlines, plenty of daylight and warm finishes that make a modern home comfortable.
