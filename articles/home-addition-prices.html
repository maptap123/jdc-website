Home Addition Prices:
What You'll Really Pay

JDC Construction · Lawrenceburg, Indiana 6 min read

Home addition prices in Southeast Indiana swing more than any other remodeling number we quote. A sunroom and a primary suite can both be called "an addition," but one costs less than a new kitchen and the other costs more than a small house.

Quick Answer

In 2026, home addition prices in Southeast Indiana generally land between $42,000 and $385,000+. A small bump-out starts around $42,000, a full primary suite typically runs $125,000 to $225,000, and a second-story addition can push past $385,000. The price depends on size, use, foundation type, and how much it ties into existing systems.

What home addition prices actually include

When homeowners in Dearborn County ask us what an addition costs, they're really asking what's inside that number. A proper price covers more than framing and drywall.

At JDC we build pricing around the full scope: engineering and permit drawings, site prep, foundation, framing, roofing, siding to match the existing house, windows, insulation, mechanicals (HVAC, electrical, plumbing), interior finishes, and the inevitable tie-ins where new meets old. The "tie-in" work is where cheap estimates fall apart.

Southeast Indiana adds its own realities. Older homes in Lawrenceburg and Aurora often need utility upgrades to carry a new load. Hillside lots common across the Cincinnati tri-state area push foundation costs higher than a flat site in a newer subdivision.

2026 home addition prices by project type

The clearest way to think about addition prices is by project type, not just square footage. A 200-square-foot bump-out and a 200-square-foot primary suite share almost nothing in cost structure.

What moves the price inside each tier

Two homeowners with the same square footage can get very different quotes. These are the drivers that explain why.

  • Foundation type. A full basement under the addition adds $30,000 to $60,000 over a slab. Crawl space falls in the middle.
  • Roofline complexity. A simple shed roof tying into an existing wall is a fraction of the cost of a hip or gable that has to blend into a complex existing roof.
  • Siding and window match. If your existing siding is discontinued or your windows have a custom size, matching them well costs more than the materials themselves.
  • HVAC capacity. If the existing system can't carry the new load, you're adding a mini-split or sizing up the main system. Plan on $4,000 to $12,000.
  • Electrical service. A primary suite with a tile shower, radiant floor, and a few circuits for ceiling fans may trigger a panel upgrade. That's $2,500 to $5,500 on its own.
  • Kitchen or bath inside the addition. Wet areas add plumbing, waterproofing, tile, and fixture costs that stack fast. A primary suite bath alone can be $35,000 to $70,000 of the total.

"The square footage number tells you the size of the box. The use inside the box is what tells you the price."

Where Southeast Indiana prices differ from the national averages

National home addition articles usually quote $200 to $500 per square foot. That's a wide range for a reason: it averages Manhattan brownstones with Indiana ranches.

In Dearborn County and the Cincinnati tri-state area, we usually see finished prices land between $275 and $525 per square foot for most additions, with primary suites and second stories pushing to the top of that range. Labor is more reasonable here than on the coasts, but the material markets we buy from (lumber, windows, cabinetry) are the same.

The biggest regional variable is permit and inspection work. Lawrenceburg, Aurora, Greendale, and the unincorporated parts of Dearborn County each handle addition permits a little differently. We handle those filings as part of the project, but they do affect schedule, which affects price.

How to get a price that actually holds

A real addition quote comes from a real scope. If someone gives you a firm price in an hour-long first visit, it's a guess.

At JDC, we usually walk the home with you, measure the tie-in points, talk through how you'll actually use the space, and come back with a budget range before we draw anything. If the range fits, we move into design and firm pricing. If it doesn't, we talk about scope changes before you've paid for drawings you can't use.
