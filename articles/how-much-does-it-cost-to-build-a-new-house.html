Building a new house in Southeast Indiana costs most homeowners between $400,000 and $900,000 in 2026. The final number depends on finish level, lot conditions, and the square footage you actually need. Here's how the math really works in Dearborn County and the Cincinnati tri-state area.

Quick Answer

A new house build in Southeast Indiana typically runs $250 to $450 per square foot in 2026, which puts most custom homes between $400,000 and $900,000 turnkey. The spread comes down to finish level, lot prep, and whether you're on a prepared subdivision lot or raw rural land.

What a New House Build Actually Includes

When homeowners ask us how much it costs to build a new house, they usually mean the finished price with everything included. That's the number you should budget to.

A turnkey new build covers four big buckets: the lot (purchase and site prep), the shell (foundation, framing, roof, windows, exterior), the systems (mechanical, electrical, plumbing), and the finishes (flooring, cabinets, countertops, fixtures, paint). Builder overhead and margin get folded in across all four.

What people often leave out: permits, impact fees, well and septic (if you're rural), driveway, landscaping, and appliances. Those easily add $30,000 to $75,000 on top of the house itself. We flag every one of these in writing before contract.

What's Different About Building in 2026

Costs stabilized through 2025 after a wild stretch, but they didn't come back down. Framing lumber is still 25% above pre-2020 pricing. Skilled trade labor in the Cincinnati tri-state area runs $65 to $95 per hour for licensed subs, which is the biggest single driver of the per-square-foot number you'll see in bids.

Three things to know if you're starting a build this year:

Windows and doors still have long lead times. Better to lock those in four to six months ahead. We order early on every project.

Appliance packages have moved up. A mid-range kitchen package is $12k to $18k now. Premium runs $25k to $40k.

Interest rates matter more than they used to. If you're financing with a construction-to-perm loan, run the numbers at a realistic rate before you lock in finish-level choices.

The Line Items That Move Your Number Most

If you want to control the final cost, these are the decisions worth spending time on. They move the number more than almost anything else.

  • Square footage. The single biggest lever. Every extra 300 sq ft adds roughly $75k-$120k depending on tier. Design tight and intentional.
  • Roof complexity. A simple gable costs a fraction of a cut-up multi-pitch roof with dormers. Big difference in framing labor and materials.
  • Foundation type. Slab is cheapest, crawl space is middle, full basement adds $35k-$75k but gives you usable finished space for $30-$50 per sq ft later.
  • Cabinet package. Stock to semi-custom is a $15k swing. Semi-custom to fully custom site-built can be $40k+ in a mid-size kitchen.
  • Window spec. Vinyl to clad-wood is $12k-$25k across a whole house, but it changes how the home looks and performs for the next 40 years.
  • HVAC system. Standard forced-air is baseline. Dual-zone, high-SEER, or geothermal adds $8k-$30k but drops operating costs long-term.

Hidden Costs Most Homeowners Miss

The listed per-square-foot price is rarely the final price. Here's what gets forgotten when people compare bids.

Site work on a raw lot. Clearing trees, grading, running utilities to the house, and putting in a driveway can easily hit $40,000 to $90,000 before you pour a footer. Lots in Dearborn County that look like a bargain often need six figures of site work.

Well and septic. If you're outside city water in rural Dearborn or Ohio County, plan on $18,000 to $35,000 combined. Soil testing comes first.

Impact fees and permits. Varies by township. Budget $4,000 to $12,000.

Final grading, driveway, landscaping. Easy to overlook, but a concrete driveway and basic landscape package is $15,000 to $35,000 by itself.

The cheapest-looking bid is almost never the cheapest finished house. We'd rather show you the real number up front than hand you a surprise six months in.

Financing a New Build

Most new house builds use a construction-to-permanent loan, which converts to a regular mortgage once the home is finished. The bank draws funds in stages as work completes, and you'll usually pay interest-only during construction.

Lenders typically want 10-20% down on the total project cost, plus proof of a signed builder contract with a detailed scope and draw schedule. We work with several regional lenders in the Cincinnati area and can point you toward loan officers who understand custom builds.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign

Before signing a contract with any builder, get clear answers on these. If a builder can't answer them plainly, that tells you something.

  • What's in the base price, and what's an allowance? Allowances are placeholders. Big ones (cabinets, flooring, lighting) often come in over budget. Ask for realistic numbers.
  • Who holds the permits and warranties? The builder should, not a sub.
  • How are change orders priced and approved? Written, signed, with a cost before the work starts. No verbal approvals.
  • What's the realistic timeline, start to move-in? Most custom homes in SE Indiana run 10 to 14 months. If someone tells you 6, ask why.
  • Can I see a finished build and talk to the homeowner? Any builder worth hiring will say yes without hesitating.
