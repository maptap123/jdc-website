Building a house is the biggest project most families ever take on. Here's the full process we walk clients through in Dearborn County and the Cincinnati tri-state area, from the first land decision to the day you move in.

Quick Answer

Building a house in Southeast Indiana takes 9 to 14 months from signed contract to move-in, and costs $250 to $400 per square foot for most custom builds. The timeline splits roughly into 3 months of design and permitting, then 6 to 11 months of construction, depending on size and complexity.

Start With the Land, Not the House Plans

The land decides more about your build than the floor plan does. Lot slope, soil, utilities, well and septic needs, and setbacks all shape what's possible and what it will cost.

We've seen homeowners fall in love with a plan, then find the lot can't take it. A walkout basement needs the right grade. A country lot needs a well, a septic system, and a longer driveway. Lots inside Lawrenceburg or Greendale usually have municipal water and sewer, which keeps site costs down.

Before you commit to a lot, walk it with a builder. Ten minutes on-site saves months of surprises. If you already own land, that's your starting point for every other decision.

The Phases of Building a House

A custom build moves through six clear phases. Each one has decisions you can't easily walk back, so understanding what happens when keeps you in control of your budget and your timeline.

  • Phase 1 — Pre-design (2 to 4 weeks). Site visit, budget conversation, builder selection, and signed design agreement.
  • Phase 2 — Design and specifications (6 to 10 weeks). Floor plans, elevations, structural details, and material selections. This is where scope gets locked.
  • Phase 3 — Permits and preconstruction (3 to 6 weeks). Dearborn County building permits, septic approval if applicable, utility coordination, and final pricing.
  • Phase 4 — Site work and foundation (3 to 5 weeks). Clearing, grading, excavation, footings, and foundation walls.
  • Phase 5 — Framing to drywall (3 to 5 months). Shell, roof, windows, mechanical rough-ins, insulation, and drywall. The house looks finished from the outside well before it is.
  • Phase 6 — Finishes and closeout (2 to 4 months). Cabinets, trim, flooring, paint, fixtures, final inspections, and the walkthrough that hands you the keys.

Weather matters here. Southeast Indiana winters slow site work and foundation pours. A build that breaks ground in April moves faster than one starting in November.

Permits, Inspections, and Dearborn County

Every new home in Dearborn County needs a building permit from the county building department, plus utility approvals, septic design if you're outside a sewer district, and sometimes zoning review. Cities like Lawrenceburg, Greendale, and Aurora have their own permit offices with their own timelines.

Count on three to six weeks between a complete submittal and permit issuance. Inspections then happen at footing, foundation, framing, rough mechanical, insulation, and final. A good builder schedules those inspections and keeps the sequence moving. A bad one lets inspections slip and burns your timeline.

Questions to Ask Before You Break Ground

Before you sign a construction contract, make sure these answers are in writing. Not a verbal promise, not an email. In the contract or in the specifications it references.

  • What's included and what's an allowance? Allowances for appliances, lighting, flooring, and plumbing fixtures are where budgets quietly inflate.
  • How are change orders handled? Written approvals, clear pricing, and a defined turnaround keep surprises off the final invoice.
  • What's the payment schedule? Draws should match completed work, not calendar dates.
  • Who manages the subs? On a JDC build, we self-perform where it counts and manage every trade on-site.
  • What's the warranty? Workmanship, systems, and structural warranties are three different things.
  • Can we see finished homes? A real builder will walk you through recent projects, not just show photos.

Building a house is a two- to three-year relationship from first conversation to the one-year warranty call. Pick the builder you want to still be talking to in year three.
