How Much Does It Cost
for a Bathroom Remodel?

JDC Construction · Lawrenceburg, Indiana 6 min read
JDC Construction › Articles › How Much Does It Cost for a Bathroom Remodel?

The cost of a bathroom remodel depends less on finishes than most homeowners think. The bigger lever is which bathroom you're remodeling — a powder room, a hall bath, a kids' bath, or a primary suite — because each one has a different footprint, a different scope of plumbing, and a different standard for what "done" looks like.

Quick Answer

In Southeast Indiana, a bathroom remodel costs between $8,000 and $55,000+ depending on which bathroom you're redoing. A half bath runs $8K to $15K, a hall or kids' bath $14K to $32K, and a primary suite bath $30K to $55K+. The room type sets the floor; finishes and layout changes push you up from there.

What drives the price (and why "it depends" is a real answer)

When homeowners ask how much it costs for a bathroom remodel, they usually get a $12K-to-$75K range back. That range isn't a dodge. It's accurate, because a half bath and a primary suite have almost nothing in common except a drain.

Three things set the real cost: which bathroom, how far you move plumbing, and finish level. Of those, the room type does the most work. A primary suite usually has a shower and a tub, double vanity, more square footage, and more tile. A powder room has a toilet, a sink, and a mirror. You can't move either one into the other's price range without crossing the room-type line.

Labor runs steady across projects in Southeast Indiana — tile setters, plumbers, and electricians charge about the same whether the room is 30 square feet or 120. What changes is how long they're on site and how much material they touch. That's why the room type is the honest starting point for an estimate.

What moves the number inside each range

Once you know the room type, four decisions push you from the low end to the high end. These are the levers you actually control.

  • Shower vs. tub vs. both. A tile shower with a curb and bench runs $4,500 to $9,000 built. A tub-shower combo with tile surround is cheaper. Keeping both in a primary suite can add $3K to $6K.
  • Tile scope. Floor only is one price. Floor plus a shower surround is more. Floor-to-ceiling tile on a wet wall or around a tub adds labor and material in a hurry.
  • Vanity choice. Stock vanity: $600 to $1,500 installed. Semi-custom with a quartz top: $2,500 to $5,000. Fully custom: $5,000 and up. Double vanities roughly double the number.
  • Moving plumbing. Keeping fixtures where they are is cheapest. Moving a toilet or shower drain means opening the floor, pulling permits, and rerouting lines. Expect $2,500 to $6,000 for layout changes.
  • Hidden conditions. On older Lawrenceburg homes we often find rotted subfloor under a shower, failed waterproofing, galvanized plumbing, or ungrounded wiring. Budget 10 to 15 percent for the surprises we only find after demo.

"The room type sets the floor. Everything else — tile, fixtures, layout — tells you how far above the floor you'll land."

Where homeowners actually underspend (and overspend)

After 30 years of remodeling bathrooms around Dearborn County, the pattern is clear. Homeowners underspend on waterproofing, ventilation, and lighting — the things nobody sees. They overspend on faucets, vessel sinks, and trendy tile patterns that date quickly.

Spend the money on the shower pan and waterproofing membrane. A $400 Schluter system behind the tile is the difference between a bathroom that lasts 25 years and one that leaks into the ceiling below in year seven. Spend on a quiet ventilation fan ducted to the outside. Spend on dimmable, layered lighting at the vanity and in the shower.

Save on the trend-driven stuff. A $90 faucet from a good brand will outlast a $400 designer faucet if the brand is right. A simple white 3x6 subway tile looks timeless and runs a quarter of what a wavy handmade zellige costs. If you're planning a project, our bathroom remodel page walks through the full scope we build.

How Dearborn County and Cincinnati-area pricing compares

Bathroom remodel pricing in Southeast Indiana is roughly 10 to 20 percent below the Cincinnati suburbs across the river. Labor rates are a touch lower here, material costs are the same, and permit fees in Dearborn County are modest compared to Hamilton County, Ohio.

That gap matters most on primary suite remodels, where total project cost is high enough that 15 percent is real money. A $45,000 primary bath in Lawrenceburg can be a $52,000 primary bath in Mason or West Chester for the same scope. The savings don't come from cutting corners — they come from a lower cost of doing business in Dearborn County.

Where you live inside Southeast Indiana doesn't change the number much. We've built bathrooms in Lawrenceburg, Aurora, Greendale, Bright, and all through the Cincinnati tri-state area at the same price points.

What a good estimate includes (and what to watch for)

A real bathroom remodel estimate should be itemized. If a bid is one lump sum with no breakdown, that's a warning. You should see line items for demo, framing, plumbing rough-in, electrical, waterproofing, tile labor, tile material, vanity, fixtures, paint, and finish work.

Watch for bids that leave out trash haul, protection of adjacent floors, or final punch-list cleanup. Watch for "fixtures by owner" clauses that sound flexible but shift risk to you. And watch for timelines that look too fast — a tile shower needs time for the thinset and grout to cure before glass goes in. Rushed waterproofing is the number one cause of callbacks in this trade.
