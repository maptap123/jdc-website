A kitchen remodel is the biggest project most homeowners ever take on inside their house. The cost swings wildly based on what you keep, what you move, and what you choose. Here is what Southeast Indiana homeowners actually pay in 2026, and why.

Quick Answer

In Southeast Indiana, a kitchen remodel typically runs $32,000 to $110,000+. A cosmetic refresh with new paint, counters, and backsplash lands near $32k–$55k. A full remodel with new cabinets, layout changes, and premium finishes runs $75k–$110k+. Moving walls or plumbing adds $8k–$20k on top.

What goes into the number

When someone asks how much it costs to remodel a kitchen, the honest answer is a range, not a single figure. The three biggest drivers are scope, cabinets, and layout changes. Everything else is secondary.

Scope decides whether you are painting a kitchen or rebuilding one. Cabinets alone can swing the budget by $20,000 or more between stock and custom. Moving a sink or opening a wall triggers plumbing, electrical, framing, and drywall work that stacks fast.

In Lawrenceburg, Dearborn County, and the Cincinnati tri-state area, labor and materials sit a little below major metro pricing but well above the online calculators that assume national averages. Kitchen remodel cost by tier

Most kitchen projects fall into one of three tiers. Picking the right tier first saves you from chasing a budget that was never realistic.

Where the money actually goes

On a typical $70,000 kitchen remodel in our area, the breakdown looks something like this. The percentages hold up at most price points, with the dollar amounts scaling up or down.

  • Cabinets: 28–35% of the total. Stock boxes save money, semi-custom hits the sweet spot, full custom pushes the top end.
  • Labor: 20–25%. Demo, framing, installation, finish carpentry. More layout changes means more labor.
  • Countertops: 8–12%. Quartz is the default now. Granite still saves a little. Butcher block and solid-surface sit between.
  • Appliances: 10–15%. A four-piece package from a mid-tier brand runs $5k–$9k. Premium brands double that.
  • Flooring: 5–8%. Luxury vinyl plank, tile, or hardwood. Material and square footage decide the total.
  • Plumbing & electrical: 5–10%. Higher if you are moving a sink, adding an island, or upgrading the panel.
  • Backsplash, lighting, hardware, paint: 5–8%. The small stuff adds up fast when you add it all together.

What drives the price up (or down)

Two kitchens the same size can land $30,000 apart depending on a handful of choices. If you are trying to control the budget, this is where the real decisions happen.

Scope changes

Opening a wall between the kitchen and living room is one of the most requested changes, and one of the priciest. Expect to add $8k–$20k once you factor in structural work, drywall, flooring repair, and finishing. If the wall is load-bearing, a beam and column work pushes the top end.

Cabinet selection

Cabinets are the single biggest line item. Stock cabinets from a big box run $8k–$15k for a typical kitchen. Semi-custom hits $18k–$32k. Full custom from a local shop can run $40k and up. The jump from stock to semi-custom is usually worth it; the jump from semi-custom to full custom is a style call, not always a quality one.

Appliance package

A full appliance package can double between a mid-tier brand like KitchenAid or GE Profile and a premium brand like Thermador or Sub-Zero. Decide what level of appliance you want before you sign off on cabinets, because the openings have to be built to fit them.

The question is never just "how much does a kitchen remodel cost." It is "how much does my kitchen cost at the finish level I want." Those are different numbers.

Hidden costs most homeowners miss

Even careful planners get surprised by a few line items. Build these into your budget from day one so they do not blow it up later.

  • Permits and inspections: $400–$1,500 depending on scope and jurisdiction.
  • Dumpster and disposal: $500–$1,200. Required on any full remodel.
  • Temporary kitchen setup: Microwave, hot plate, mini-fridge in a dining room for 2–4 months. Call it $300–$600 plus the dining-out budget.
  • Old-house surprises: On any home built before 1990, plan a 10–15% contingency for knob-and-tube wiring, cast-iron drain lines, or framing that is not quite plumb.
  • Design and selections: Showroom trips, 3D renderings, sample ordering. Built into our fixed-price quotes, but worth asking about.
  • Sales tax on materials: Indiana sales tax applies to purchased materials, which can add 7% to that portion of the project.

How to keep the number realistic

If you want a predictable price, work with a contractor who will scope the project tightly before quoting it. Allowances and vague line items are where remodels blow up. A clear, itemized quote with real brand and model numbers is the single best predictor of hitting budget.
