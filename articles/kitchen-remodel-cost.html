Kitchen Remodel Cost:
A Line-by-Line Breakdown.

JDC Construction · Lawrenceburg, Indiana 6 min read
JDC Construction › Articles › Kitchen Remodel Cost

Most kitchen remodel cost estimates lump everything into one big number. Here is what actually drives that number in Southeast Indiana homes, line by line, with real ranges you can take to a lender or a spouse.

Quick Answer

A full kitchen remodel in Southeast Indiana typically runs $30,000 to $95,000+, with cabinets ($12K to $45K) and labor ($10K to $25K) as the two biggest line items. Countertops, appliances, plumbing, electrical, and flooring each add $2K to $12K depending on scope and finish level.

Why kitchen remodel cost ranges are so wide

Two kitchens the same size can finish $40,000 apart. The gap is not usually luxury versus budget. It is scope.

A paint-and-swap refresh leaves cabinets, layout, and plumbing where they are. A gut remodel moves walls, re-pipes, re-wires, and replaces every surface. Both are called "kitchen remodels." Only one of them has a $20K line item for cabinets.

At JDC Construction we have been remodeling kitchens in Dearborn County and the Cincinnati tri-state area since 1996. The number that matters is not the headline average. It is the sum of your specific line items.

The seven line items that drive your total

Every kitchen remodel estimate we write breaks into roughly the same seven categories. Here is what each one typically runs for a 150 to 220 square-foot kitchen in Southeast Indiana.

Cabinets

Range: $12,000 to $45,000. Cabinets are the single biggest line item in almost every kitchen remodel cost. Stock cabinets from a big-box store run $6K to $12K for a mid-size kitchen but tend to look it after five years. Semi-custom boxes with soft-close hardware and real wood doors land between $15K and $28K and hit the sweet spot for most homeowners. Fully custom from a local shop can push past $45K, especially with inset doors or specialty finishes.

Countertops

Range: $3,000 to $10,000. Quartz is the default for a reason: it looks like stone, resists stains, and runs $60 to $100 per square foot installed. Granite is comparable. Butcher block, laminate, or solid surface will come in lower. Full-slab natural quartzite or marble can double the quartz number fast.

Appliances

Range: $3,000 to $15,000. A straight-swap package (range, dishwasher, microwave, refrigerator) in a standard brand runs $3K to $5K. Step up to a quieter dishwasher, induction range, and counter-depth fridge and you are at $7K to $10K. A full pro-style package with a gas range, hood, and built-in refrigerator can clear $15K before you factor in the extra cabinetry that frames it.

Labor and project management

Range: $10,000 to $25,000. This covers demo, carpentry, installation, finish work, project management, and the trades we coordinate on your behalf. On most JDC jobs, labor runs 25 to 35 percent of the total. The variable here is complexity: a straight pull-and-replace is less labor than a wall removal and layout change.

Plumbing

Range: $1,500 to $6,000. If the sink stays put and you are reusing supply lines and drain, this is a few hundred dollars for fixture install. Move the sink to a new wall, add a prep sink or pot filler, and you are closer to $4K to $6K after the plumber reroutes supply and drain. Older Lawrenceburg homes with galvanized or cast-iron lines often need upgrades we uncover during demo.

Electrical

Range: $1,500 to $6,000. Code requires GFCI outlets at countertops, dedicated circuits for the dishwasher and disposal, and arc-fault protection throughout. Add under-cabinet lighting, recessed cans, pendants over an island, and a new fixture over the sink, and the electrical bill climbs. A full re-wire of a 1970s kitchen with an undersized panel can mean a panel upgrade on top.

Flooring, drywall, paint, and finish

Range: $2,500 to $8,000. New flooring in a 200 sq ft kitchen typically runs $1,500 to $4,000 depending on material (LVP on the low end, hardwood or tile on the high end). Drywall patch and repair after cabinet changes, trim, and two coats of paint add another $1,500 to $4,000. Backsplash tile sits here too, usually $600 to $2,500 installed.

What makes your kitchen remodel cost swing within a tier

Two homeowners can both land in Tier 2 and still see a $15K spread. These are the usual culprits.

  • Layout changes. Moving the sink, range, or refrigerator five feet is a meaningful plumbing and electrical bill. Keeping the triangle where it is saves $3K to $8K.
  • Hidden conditions. Older Lawrenceburg and Aurora homes often reveal knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, or sub-floor rot behind the dishwasher. Our contingency allowance covers these.
  • Cabinet reach. Taller cabinets, pantry towers, and a bigger island all mean more linear feet, and cabinets price by the foot.
  • Countertop fabrication. A single-slab waterfall island with mitered edges is three times the fabrication of a straight run with a square edge.
  • Appliance package. A $5K package and a $15K package install the same way. The $10K difference is pure line-item.
  • Timing. Jumping the schedule to beat the holidays or a baby due date usually means overtime and a tighter trade sequence.

How to lock in a realistic number before you sign

A good estimate is specific. Vague is where surprises come from. Here is what we recommend you ask any contractor before you compare numbers.

  • Ask for a line-item estimate. Any contractor should break out cabinets, countertops, appliances, labor, plumbing, electrical, flooring, and contingency. If it is one lump number, that is not an estimate, it is a guess.
  • Confirm the allowance figures. Allowances are placeholders for choices you have not made yet (tile, fixtures, hardware). Make sure the allowance is realistic for the look you actually want.
  • Pin down the appliance package. Model numbers, not "appliance allowance $5,000." The number of homeowners who find out the quoted fridge does not fit the quoted cabinet opening is higher than it should be.
  • Build in a 10 to 15 percent contingency. Not for scope creep, for what demo uncovers in a 60-year-old home.
  • Get the schedule in writing. Start date, major milestones, substantial completion. A good contractor will not flinch at putting dates on paper.
