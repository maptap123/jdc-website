A kitchen remodel is one of the biggest investments most Southeast Indiana homeowners make. The budget range is wide for a reason, and the number a contractor gives you should still hold up when the walls come off.

Quick Answer

Most kitchen remodels in Southeast Indiana run $25,000 to $100,000+, with the sweet spot for a full-scope project landing around $55,000 to $85,000. The final number depends on cabinet grade, countertop material, layout changes, and the condition of what is behind the walls.

Why Kitchen Remodel Cost Varies So Much

Two kitchens the same size can land $40,000 apart. That is not a markup problem. It is a scope problem.

A refresh with new cabinets, countertops, and appliances is a different animal from a full gut remodel that moves a wall, upgrades the electrical panel, and reroutes plumbing. Both are legitimate projects. They just come with different price tags.

In Dearborn County and the broader Cincinnati tri-state area, we see three distinct kitchen remodel cost tiers. Where your project lands depends mostly on three things: your cabinets, your layout, and the condition of what is behind the walls.

What Actually Drives Kitchen Remodel Cost

Four line items account for most of the swing between a $45,000 kitchen and a $90,000 kitchen. Walk through each with your contractor before you pick finishes.

Cabinets

Cabinets are typically 30 to 40 percent of the total. Stock cabinets land around $4,000 to $9,000 for an average kitchen. Semi-custom runs $12,000 to $25,000. Custom-built cabinets can hit $30,000 to $50,000 or more. The jump between stock and semi-custom is usually worth it, because soft-close doors, better box construction, and better finishes hold up longer.

Countertops

Expect $3,000 to $12,000 depending on material and square footage. Laminate is cheapest. Quartz has become the default in the mid tier because it is durable, consistent, and does not need sealing. High-end stone like exotic granite, soapstone, or honed marble adds $4,000 to $8,000 over quartz.

Layout Changes

Moving a sink two feet is $1,500. Taking down a load-bearing wall is $8,000 to $20,000. Relocating a gas line, adding a window, or bumping out a wall are the choices that push a Tier 2 kitchen into Tier 3 fast. If the layout truly works for you, keep it.

What Is Behind the Walls

Older homes in Lawrenceburg and across Dearborn County often need electrical panel upgrades, GFCI circuits, better insulation, or outdated plumbing replaced. We budget $3,000 to $12,000 for mechanical surprises on homes built before 1990. It is not fun money, but it is honest money.

The cheapest kitchen remodel is the one you only build once. Spend where it will show for twenty years.

Where Kitchen Remodels Go Over Budget

Overruns are predictable. We see the same handful of things again and again across Southeast Indiana projects.

  • Change orders mid-project. Picking a new tile at week three means the schedule stops and the labor meter keeps running. Lock finish selections before demo day.
  • Surprises behind the walls. Rotted subfloor, knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing. Budget a 10 to 15 percent contingency on any home over 30 years old.
  • Appliance creep. A $4,500 appliance package becomes a $12,000 package once you start adding steam ovens and column fridges. Set the budget before you walk into the showroom.
  • Light fixtures and hardware. These feel small but add up quickly. $150 pendants become $600 pendants. Pick them early and stick to the number.
  • Permit and inspection delays. Not a cost item exactly, but a timeline item that costs money. Plan around the Dearborn County inspection schedule, not a wishful one.

Where to Spend and Where to Save

The best kitchens we build spend heavy on the bones and modestly on the finishes you can swap out later.

Spend on: cabinets, countertops, the layout itself, and electrical and plumbing done once and done right. These are the things that are expensive to redo later.

Save on: trend-driven paint colors, hardware, pendant lights, and backsplash tile. A $4 subway tile and an $18 porcelain tile both look sharp. Hardware swaps are a Saturday afternoon.
