How Long to Build
a Custom Home?

By August Cox · JDC Construction · Lawrenceburg, Indiana 5 min read
JDC Construction › Articles › How Long Does It Take to Build a Custom Home in Southeast Indiana?

Building a custom home is the biggest project most families ever take on. The timeline question comes up first, before design, before land, before anything else.

Quick Answer

A custom home in Southeast Indiana typically takes 10 to 18 months from signed contract to move-in. Smaller, straightforward builds can finish in 9 months. Complex designs, custom-milled finishes, or difficult sites push closer to 20 months.

The Short Answer

Most custom homes in Southeast Indiana take 10 to 18 months from the day you sign a contract to the day you get the keys. That's a wide range on purpose. A 2,400 square foot ranch on flat land in Dearborn County can wrap in 9 or 10 months. A larger two-story with custom millwork, a walk-out basement, or a tricky hillside site can stretch to 18 or even 20.

The clock starts earlier than most people think. Land search, financing, and the first design conversations add another 3 to 6 months on the front end. If you want to be in your new home by Christmas 2027, you should be talking to a builder now.

Phase by Phase: Where the Time Really Goes

Here's how a typical JDC Construction custom home breaks down after you sign a contract. We've been building homes in Dearborn County since 1996, and the phases below are where the months actually go.

Phase 1: Planning and Design (2 to 4 months)

This is where most of the heavy lifting happens. You'll work with our designer on the floor plan, lock in elevations, and specify exterior materials. Interior selections, cabinets, countertops, tile, flooring, lighting, plumbing fixtures, all get pinned down now, before a single footer is dug.

The homes that run smoothly in the field are the ones where owners did the work up front. Changes made during framing cost roughly three times what they would cost during design.

Phase 2: Permits and Pre-Construction (1 to 2 months)

Once drawings are stamped, we submit for building permits with Dearborn County or the local jurisdiction. HVAC, plumbing, and electrical trades get scheduled. The site gets surveyed and staked. Utilities (well, septic, electric service) get ordered. This is quiet time for you, but it's critical sequencing for us.

Phase 3: Site Work and Foundation (3 to 6 weeks)

Excavation, footers, foundation walls, waterproofing, backfill. A walk-out basement adds 2 to 3 weeks over a slab or crawlspace. Weather matters here. A wet spring in Southeast Indiana can slow things by a week or two, and we build that into the schedule.

Phase 4: Framing and Shell (2 to 3 months)

Walls go up. The roof gets dried in. Windows and exterior doors go in. Plumbing, HVAC, and electrical rough-ins happen. Insulation and drywall follow. This is the most visible phase. You drive by and see real progress every week.

Phase 5: Interior and Finishing (3 to 5 months)

Cabinets, countertops, tile, flooring, trim, paint, lighting, plumbing fixtures, final HVAC. Then driveway, landscaping, final grade, and the punch list. This phase takes longer than most homeowners expect because every trade has to come back for a final visit.

The homes that run smoothly in the field are the ones where owners did the work up front.

What Slows a Southeast Indiana Build Down

A few things we see push timelines consistently. If you want to stay on schedule, these are the places to watch.

  • Changes made after framing. Moving a wall or window after drywall adds days and dollars. We push to lock decisions early.
  • Long-lead items. Custom windows, specialty tile, and imported cabinetry can carry 12 to 20 week lead times. We order these as soon as drawings are stamped.
  • Permit timing. Dearborn County is generally quick, but outlying jurisdictions and septic approvals can add weeks.
  • Weather. A wet Southeast Indiana spring can slow excavation and foundation work. We plan around it, but it still shows up.
  • Financing holdups. Construction loan draws have to clear on time. A slow bank can stall a trade for a week.

How to Keep Your Project on Schedule

The homes that finish on time share a few habits. Finalize every interior selection before construction starts. Use a builder who communicates weekly, in writing, so you are never surprised. Choose finishes that are in stock, not drop-shipped from overseas. And pick a builder whose trades actually show up. One of the biggest timeline killers is a subcontractor who drags.
