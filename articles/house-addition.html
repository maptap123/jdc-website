House Addition:
What It Really Takes to Add On.

A house addition is one of the biggest decisions a homeowner can make. More square footage, better flow, a kitchen that finally works. The upside is real. So is the complexity. Here's what to know before you add on in Southeast Indiana.

Quick Answer

A house addition is any permanent expansion of your home's conditioned footprint, from a small bump-out to a full second-story build. In Southeast Indiana, projects typically run $85,000 to $375,000+, depending on size, finish level, and whether you're adding living space over a new foundation or building up over existing framing.

What Counts as a House Addition?

Not every expansion counts as a true house addition. We get calls every month from homeowners in Dearborn County asking about adding square footage, and the first conversation is usually about what they're actually building.

A house addition is any permanent expansion of your home's conditioned footprint. That means new foundation, new framing, new roofing if needed, extended mechanicals, and finished interior space you heat and cool year-round.

A deck doesn't qualify. A screened porch on a slab usually doesn't. A finished basement doesn't either, because you're not expanding the footprint. A sunroom on a full foundation, tied into your HVAC? That counts.

Four Types of House Additions

In Southeast Indiana, the house additions we build fall into four buckets. Each one solves a different problem.

  • Bump-outs. 30 to 150 square feet, usually cantilevered or set on a small foundation. Perfect for stealing room for a breakfast nook, a larger shower, or a reading corner off a bedroom.
  • Full room additions. One or more rooms added to the side or back of the home on a new foundation. The most common ask: a primary suite or a bigger family room.
  • Second-story additions. Building up over the existing single-story footprint. Usually driven by a growing family and a lot that can't spare the yard.
  • In-law or accessory suites. A bedroom, full bath, and sometimes a small kitchenette. Often for an aging parent or a returning adult child.

The House Addition Process, Start to Finish

Every addition we build in the Cincinnati area follows roughly the same arc. Phases blur together on the jobsite, but the order matters. Homeowners who understand the sequence make better decisions along the way.

  • Design and feasibility. Site review, rough concept, what your lot and local codes will allow before any drawings get paid for.
  • Architectural drawings. Full construction documents the county will approve and the trades can build from.
  • Permitting. Dearborn County or city of Lawrenceburg, plus any floodplain, zoning, or health-department reviews.
  • Foundation and framing. The slowest visible phase and the most weather-dependent one.
  • Mechanicals, drywall, and finish work. Plumbing, electrical, and HVAC rough-ins, then trim, paint, flooring, and fixtures.
  • Final inspections and walkthrough. Punch list, final county sign-off, and handoff.

Start to finish, most JDC additions take 4 to 9 months from signed contract to final walkthrough. Larger second-story projects can run 10 to 14 months, especially if the design phase surfaces a structural question that needs an engineer.

Will Your Lot Support a House Addition?

Before design starts, we look hard at the lot. In Dearborn County, setbacks, drainage, and zoning all affect what you can legally build. If you're inside Lawrenceburg city limits, there are additional reviews. If you're in a floodplain near the Ohio or Whitewater rivers, FEMA elevation requirements come into play.

A house addition isn't a fast project. The homeowners who are happiest at the end are the ones who gave the design phase enough time to get it right.

We walk the lot and pull the zoning maps before anyone pays for architectural drawings. There's no point designing something the county won't permit.

Questions to Ask Before You Add On

Before you sign anything, run through this short list. Honest answers save money and months.

  • Is adding on cheaper than moving? Not always. Run a comp on comparable homes in your area first.
  • Does your lot allow it? Setbacks, easements, and the local zoning code decide, not the builder.
  • Can your existing systems handle it? HVAC capacity, electrical panel size, and septic or sewer load all matter.
  • Who does the structural engineering? A licensed engineer, not a sales rep with a template.
  • Is the builder licensed, insured, and local? JDC has been building in Southeast Indiana since 1996 and was named Indiana Remodeler of the Year in 2019.

Should You Add On, Build New, or Move?

The honest answer is: it depends on what you have now.

If you love your lot and your neighborhood and the existing house is structurally sound, an addition usually pencils out. If the house has serious issues, like foundation cracks, failing roof, outdated electrical, or undersized HVAC, the math can flip toward either a whole-home remodel or a tear-down rebuild.

We walk through this with every homeowner before anyone talks about design. The goal is the right project for your lot and your budget, not the biggest one.
