House Addition Cost:
What You'll Actually Pay.

A house addition is one of the biggest investments a homeowner makes, and the cost swing is wide. Here's a clear breakdown of house addition cost in Southeast Indiana for 2026, by project type, by square foot, and by the real line items that drive the number.

Quick Answer

In Southeast Indiana, house addition cost ranges from $45,000 to $350,000+ in 2026, depending on size, finish level, and whether the addition includes a bathroom, kitchen, or second story. Expect $225 to $425 per square foot for most projects in Dearborn County and the Cincinnati tri-state area. Simple bump-outs land at the low end. Full suite or second-story additions land at the top.

House Addition Cost by Project Type

Searching "house addition cost" pulls up a dozen different projects under one number. A 100 square foot bump-out and a full second-story addition are not the same build, and the price gap between them is huge.

We break house additions into three honest tiers based on what we're actually building across Dearborn County, Lawrenceburg, and the greater Cincinnati tri-state area. These tiers reflect 2026 labor and material pricing in our market, not national averages that miss by 20 percent either way.

Tier 1
Bump-Out or Small Room Addition

Under 300 sq ft. No new plumbing. Single story. Usually a sunroom, mudroom, dining expansion, or a kitchen bump-out of a few feet. Builder-grade finishes and a single HVAC supply.

Tier 2
Full Room, Bath, or Primary Suite

300 to 600 sq ft. Often includes a new bathroom, walk-in closet, or expanded kitchen. New foundation, roof tie-in, upgraded trim and tile. The most common house addition we build.

Second-Story or Large Multi-Room

600+ sq ft. Second-story additions, in-law suites with a full kitchen, or large ground-floor builds. Structural reinforcement of the existing home. Premium cabinetry and finishes.

What Actually Drives House Addition Cost

Two additions with the same square footage can land $80,000 to $100,000 apart. The structural stuff you can't see from the curb is where most of the money goes.

Foundation Type

A new addition needs a new foundation, and the type matters a lot. A slab is the cheapest option, but Southeast Indiana frost depth and soil conditions usually push us toward a crawl space or full basement for anything tied into the main house. Crawl space adds roughly $15,000 to $30,000 over a slab. A full basement under the addition can add $30,000 to $60,000, though it also gives you new usable square footage.

Plumbing, Electrical, and HVAC

Any addition that adds a bathroom, kitchen, or laundry pulls in real plumbing cost: new supply and drain lines, sometimes a water heater upgrade, and occasionally a septic review in Dearborn County rural areas. Figure $12,000 to $30,000 for plumbing scope on a Tier 2 addition.

HVAC behaves similarly. A small bump-out may only need a mini-split. A 500 square foot addition usually needs the main furnace and AC reviewed, and often supplemented. Electrical can surprise you too. Older Southeast Indiana homes often have 100 or 150 amp panels, and a new addition can push you over code, requiring a $2,500 to $5,000 panel upgrade.

Roof Tie-In and Second Stories

Tying a new roofline into an existing one is more work than it looks. Done right, it's seamless and watertight for decades. Done cheap, it leaks within three winters. Second-story additions compound this: the existing roof is removed, the walls below get engineering review, and the home has to be weather-protected for several weeks while the new structure goes up.

Finish Level

Cabinets, flooring, trim, tile, and fixtures usually account for 25 to 35 percent of the total house addition cost. The jump from builder-grade to semi-custom is often $20,000 to $45,000 on a mid-size project. This is the easiest place to protect or stretch your budget without redesigning the structural plan.

House addition cost almost always lives in three questions: what foundation, does it need plumbing, and are you going up or out?

Hidden Costs Most House Addition Bids Miss

Low estimates are usually low because they're leaving things out. These are the line items we see missing from competitor bids most often, especially on quick quotes done over the phone.

  • Permits and plan review. Dearborn County permits for a house addition run $800 to $2,500 depending on scope. Many bids list this as "to be determined" and absorb it badly later.
  • Structural engineering. Additions over 400 sq ft and nearly all second-story builds need a stamped engineer's plan. Budget $1,500 to $4,000.
  • Matching exterior. Siding, roof shingles, and brick that match a 15 to 25 year old home are harder to source than you'd expect. Sometimes we re-side or repaint the full wall to blend the new addition in.
  • Interior patch-in work. Cutting into an existing wall almost always means repainting the adjoining room, patching drywall, and refinishing floors where the new space meets the old.
  • Utility relocations. Buried electrical, gas, or water lines sometimes have to move before footings can go in. We budget for it when we see it on the walkthrough.
  • Living adjustments during the build. Not an invoice line, but a real cost: temporary dust walls, access routing, sometimes relocating a bedroom or kitchen function for a few weeks.

Is a House Addition Worth It vs. Moving?

For most Southeast Indiana homeowners who need more space, yes, and usually by a lot. Moving to a comparable larger home in Dearborn County right now typically means 6 to 8 percent of the sale price in realtor fees, moving costs, and new-home closing expenses, plus a higher mortgage rate than most people have locked in today.

On a $400,000 home, that's $25,000 to $32,000 in transaction costs alone before you've gained a single square foot. A $150,000 house addition keeps your current rate, your property tax base, your neighborhood, and your school district. For many families we work with in Lawrenceburg and the Cincinnati area, the math is obvious once it's laid out side by side.

How to Get an Honest House Addition Cost Number

Get at least two detailed estimates. Ask every builder to break out foundation, framing, roofing, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, and finishes as separate line items. If one bid comes in dramatically cheaper, it's almost always missing scope, not finding a secret.

Walk the property with each builder. A real quote comes from standing in the space, looking at the roofline, checking the panel, and understanding how the new structure will tie into the existing home. A number pulled from square footage alone will be off, sometimes by $50,000 or more.
