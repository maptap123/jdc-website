Homeowners in Southeast Indiana keep asking the same question. Where does the money actually go in a bathroom remodel? Here is a line-by-line answer, based on the projects we have run out of Lawrenceburg since 1996.

Quick Answer

A full bathroom remodel in Southeast Indiana typically runs $15,000 to $45,000, with labor accounting for about 35 to 45 percent of the total. The gap between the low and high end comes down to fixture choices, whether the layout changes, and what is hidden behind the walls once demo starts.

Where the Money Actually Goes

Every bathroom is different, but the cost buckets are usually the same. Here is roughly how a $30,000 mid-range bathroom remodel in Dearborn County breaks down when we build the estimate.

  • Labor (35 to 45 percent): $10,500 to $13,500. Demo, framing, plumbing, electrical, tile setting, drywall, and finish carpentry. Skilled trades are always the largest single line.
  • Cabinetry and vanity (10 to 15 percent): $3,000 to $4,500. Stock cabinets sit at the bottom, semi-custom in the middle, custom-built vanities push higher.
  • Tile and stone (10 to 15 percent): $3,000 to $4,500. Ceramic on the low end, natural stone or large-format porcelain on the high end.
  • Plumbing fixtures (8 to 12 percent): $2,400 to $3,600. Toilet, sink, faucets, and shower valve. Add more if you want a soaking tub or a body-spray shower system.
  • Countertops (5 to 8 percent): $1,500 to $2,400. Quartz has become the default in our market for good reason.
  • Lighting and electrical (4 to 6 percent): $1,200 to $1,800. New circuits, vanity lights, exhaust fan, GFCI outlets.
  • Permits and inspections (2 to 3 percent): $600 to $900 in Dearborn County when the job triggers plumbing or electrical permits.
  • Contingency (5 to 10 percent): $1,500 to $3,000. What you hold back for the surprises we cover below.

The Line Items People Underestimate

The categories above are the ones most homeowners already have on their spreadsheet. Here are the ones we see people miss when they try to build a bathroom budget on their own.

Layout and plumbing moves

Sliding the toilet three feet or moving a shower drain is not a $200 change. Once you break concrete or open a joist bay, you are adding labor, materials, and inspection time. Expect $1,500 to $5,000 for meaningful layout changes.

Waterproofing and subfloor prep

A cheap bathroom remodel skips the membrane. A remodel that lasts twenty years does not. A proper Schluter or equivalent waterproofing system on a walk-in shower adds $400 to $800 in materials plus a full day of skilled labor. Worth every dollar you spend on it.

Trim and finish carpentry

The line that shows up quietly at the end. Baseboards, door casings, closet build-outs, and any custom shelving can add $500 to $2,500 depending on what you are asking for.

The bathrooms that come in over budget are almost never the ones where the tile costs more. They are the ones where something behind the wall changes the plan.

What Actually Changes Your Total

Two homeowners in Lawrenceburg with the same square footage can end up with wildly different bathroom quotes. Four factors do most of the work.

Cost Driver 1
Bathroom size

A 40-square-foot powder room shares almost nothing with a 120-square-foot primary bath. More square feet means more tile, more framing, and more surface prep. This is the single biggest variable.

$5K - $15K Impact on total
Cost Driver 2
Fixture quality

A big-box toilet is $250. A pressure-assisted, elongated, comfort-height unit from a plumbing supply house can hit $900. Multiply that difference across every fixture in the room.

$2K - $8K Impact on total
Cost Driver 3
Layout changes

Keep everything where it is and your plumbing costs stay low. Move the toilet and shower to opposite walls and you are paying for new drain lines, fresh venting, and another round of inspection.

$1.5K - $5K Impact on total
Cost Driver 4
Hidden issues

Rot behind the shower pan. A leaking wax ring that ate the subfloor. Aluminum wiring from a 1970s remodel. These are always in play on older homes across Dearborn County and the Cincinnati tri-state area.

$500 - $6K Impact on total
