Craftsman Modern-Farmhouse Estate

A 5,300-square-foot Craftsman and modern-farmhouse custom home near Guilford, Indiana, with a finished walkout lower level and home theater.

The largest home in our portfolio

This Craftsman and modern-farmhouse home near Guilford is the biggest custom home on our portfolio page, at more than 5,300 finished square feet across three levels. The exterior combines stone, board-and-batten siding and timber details, and a three-bay garage handles the vehicles.

Inside, the house is full of color, texture and natural materials: warm wood cabinets, dark stone, sage green and red walls, and big black-framed windows that look out on the country around it. This page walks through what makes it work.

A great room built around stone and timber

The great room is the heart of the house. A floor-to-ceiling stone fireplace in dark, rugged stone anchors one wall, flanked by open floating shelves, and a low wood hearth bench wraps the base. Timber beams cross a coffered ceiling, with a large ceiling fan hung from the center.

On the far wall, a bank of black-framed windows frames the view outdoors and floods the room with natural light. The wood-look plank flooring and sage green walls keep the space feeling warm rather than formal, and the room opens through wide cased openings to the foyer and the kitchen.

A kitchen in natural wood and sage green

The kitchen steps away from the all-white look. Natural light-wood cabinets run up to the ceiling, with a sage green island topped in a pale, veined stone-look surface. A stained wood range hood, slate-toned tile backsplash with accent tiles and lantern-style pendant lights over the island give the room texture and a lot of character.

Tall cabinets run along one wall, and a long run of countertop gives several people room to cook at once. The island is large enough to gather around, and the kitchen sits right beside the great room.

A home office and a laundry room that work as hard as you do

A built-in office has floor-to-ceiling white shelving, a deep red accent wall and a long desk with a rich wood top, a space that works as a home office, a craft room or a homework station. A laundry room with sunny yellow walls, a wood shelf above a front-loading washer and dryer set on pedestals, and a window with a view makes laundry day a bit less of a chore.

A primary suite with a spa-style bath

The primary suite is on the main floor and is built for relaxation. The bath combines a large freestanding soaking tub with a walk-in tile shower, set in sage green walls with big windows and a vanity in the same natural wood used in the kitchen. The shower is finished in large-format gray tile with a built-in niche and a built-in bench, and a handheld sprayer sits beside the rain head.

Next to the bath, a dressing area has a large white island with drawers and floor-to-ceiling shelving, so everything has a place.

Color, from top to bottom

One of the first things you notice is how much color this house uses. The powder room has a bold red wall and a dark vessel sink on a wood vanity. The stairway has a stained wood newel post and black iron balusters against green walls. Upstairs and down, rooms are painted in teal, red, yellow and blue.

Color is a design choice that is easy to change later, and these rooms were planned so the owners' personality could show.

A walkout lower level made for hosting

The finished walkout lower level adds 1,878 square feet, which is more than most entire houses have to spare. A large, open recreation room is set up around a full kitchenette with gray cabinets, a stainless refrigerator and a long walnut counter that doubles as a bar or buffet. Glass doors open to the outdoors.

A home theater, additional carpeted rooms with bold accent walls in teal and red, and a bath with a tiled walk-in shower make the lower level a self-contained guest floor. With the kitchenette, it could host a party without anyone going upstairs.

Planning something on this scale?

Homes at this scale take careful planning, and punch lists run long. Some exterior and finishing work continued after the family moved in. If you are thinking about a large custom home, we would be glad to talk through your lot, your program and what a realistic budget looks like.
