Finding Land to Build a Home in Southeast Indiana

A good lot makes a better house and a better budget. Here is what to check before you buy, and how we help you decide.

Buy the lot with the house in mind

The most expensive land mistakes happen before a builder is involved: a lot that looks perfect but needs a costly septic system, a long utility run, a retaining wall or a driveway that is hard to build. Each of these can add real money to a project, and each can be checked before you sign.

We help families evaluate land before they buy it. We work with several local real estate partners, and we are also happy to build on a lot you have already chosen. You do not need to own land to start a conversation with us.

What to check before you buy

This is the short list we walk through on a site visit. A seller or agent may not raise these, so it is worth asking about each one.

  • Sewer or septic
  • Water supply
  • Electric and internet
  • Road access and driveway
  • Slope and drainage
  • Flood zone
  • Soil conditions
  • Setbacks and zoning
  • Easements
  • Covenants or association rules

Sewer, septic and water

Not every lot is on public sewer. Where it is not, a septic system needs a permit from the county health department, which starts with a soil evaluation by a state-recognized soil scientist. We recommend checking sewer or septic before you buy a lot, because it can affect both the house plan and the budget.

Ask whether public water is available at the road or whether the lot would need a well. Both are workable, but they change the budget and sometimes the placement of the house.

Access, utilities and the driveway

A lot can be cheap for a reason. If power has to be brought a long way, or the driveway has to cross a creek or climb a steep grade, that cost belongs in your decision. We estimate these items on the site visit so that you can compare lots on what they will really cost to build on, not just the asking price.

Slope, drainage and flood zones

A sloping lot is not a problem. It is often a good fit for a walkout lower level, and several of our recent homes use one. A lot that collects water is a different matter, and so is one inside a mapped floodplain. You can look up flood maps for an address on FEMA’s Flood Map Service Center, and we check them as a matter of routine for lots near the Ohio River and its creeks.

For soil, the USDA’s Web Soil Survey is a free tool that gives a general picture of what is under a parcel. It does not replace a site-specific evaluation, but it is a useful first look.

Setbacks, zoning and approvals

Dearborn County’s building department issues building permits across much of the county, while your city or town may handle zoning and location approvals separately. We confirm the exact path for your address before drawings are final, so there are no surprises at the permit counter.

Setbacks, easements and any covenants or association rules decide where on the lot the house can actually go. We read these against your plan before you commit, so you know how much of the lot is truly buildable.

Budget for land and home together

Land is only one line in the total. Set the budget for the lot, site work, house and permits together, so that a bargain lot does not leave too little for the home you wanted. Questions about buying land to build on

Can you help me find land to build on?

We help you evaluate lots you are considering, and we work with several local real estate partners. We are also glad to build on a site you have already selected.

What should I check before buying a lot?

Sewer or septic, water supply, electric access, road access and the driveway, slope and drainage, flood zone, soil, setbacks and zoning, easements, and any covenants or association rules.

Will I need a septic system?

Many rural lots are not on public sewer. A septic system needs a county health department permit and a soil evaluation by a state-recognized soil scientist. We recommend checking this before you buy.

Can you look at a lot with me before I buy it?

Yes. Send us the address or parcel and we will walk the property with you and give you an honest view of what it will take to build there.
