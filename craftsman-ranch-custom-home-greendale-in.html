Craftsman-Style Ranch with a Walkout Lower Level

A Craftsman-influenced custom ranch in Greendale, Indiana, with a walkout lower level, timber details and a covered rear deck.

92 photos

A ranch designed around the walkout

This Craftsman-influenced home in Greendale was planned around a simple idea: live on one level, and let the lower level open straight onto the yard. The main floor holds the kitchen, great room, primary suite and a flexible office that can double as a bedroom. Below it, a finished walkout adds another 1,210 square feet, for 3,274 finished square feet in all.

From the first day of construction to occupancy took about seven months in 2024. What follows is a room-by-room look at how the house came together.

Timber, stone and board-and-batten

The front elevation combines gray-blue board-and-batten siding with stone accents and a deep covered porch carried on heavy timber posts. A pair of glass-paneled wood doors anchors the entry, and stained wood garage doors repeat the warm timber tone from the porch.

Landscape beds, a paved walkway and a porch big enough for rocking chairs make the entry feel settled into the site rather than perched on it.

An open great room and kitchen

Inside, the main living space is a single open room. A stone fireplace anchors the great room, exposed timber beams run overhead, and a wall of black-framed windows pulls in views of the rolling green landscape beyond. The beams tie the interior back to the timber on the porch outside.

The great room flows directly into the kitchen, so whoever is cooking is never cut off from the room. A wrought-iron stair rail marks the way down to the lower level without closing anything off.

A kitchen with two tones and plenty of storage

The kitchen is built around a large island with a dark wood base and a light stone-look top. Painted cabinetry in soft blue and warm white sits against a mosaic tile backsplash, with a farmhouse-style apron sink, a range under a stained wood hood and black fixtures throughout.

The details are practical as well as pretty. A pull-out trash cabinet sits in the island, a slim pull-out spice drawer keeps the cooktop area tidy, and a built-in window bench in blue opens up for storage while giving the breakfast area a place to sit with a view.

Built-ins, color and a pantry that works

Rather than keep everything white, this home uses color in its cabinetry. The same soft blue appears on the kitchen cabinets, the pantry wall, the window bench and a gray-blue built-in bookcase with a warm wood top. Because the color repeats, it reads as a deliberate palette and not a one-off accent.

A tall blue pantry wall with a butcher-block work counter and open shelving keeps small appliances and groceries within a few steps of the range.

A mudroom and laundry that earn their space

The mudroom is lined with tall blue lockers, a wood bench and a bright yellow utility sink, a cheerful and practical spot for coats, boots and muddy hands. The laundry room switches to sage green, with a full counter for folding and plenty of cabinet space around the washer and dryer.

A primary suite with a spa-style bath

The primary suite sits on the main level. A sliding barn door opens into the bath, where a freestanding soaking tub sits on a patterned hexagon tile floor. A double vanity in a light wood finish runs along one wall, and a walk-in shower is finished in marble-look tile with a built-in bench.

The walk-in closet is fitted with custom shelving, drawers and hanging rods, so the room is organized from the day the owners move in. A second bath takes a different approach, with a gray vanity, a tub-shower and a pebble tile floor.

A finished lower level that walks out to the yard

The lower level is a second living floor. A family room with its own stone fireplace opens to the covered patio, and a bar area with a wood countertop and a hammered copper sink makes entertaining easy. A wall of walnut-toned built-in shelving with accent lighting gives the room a library feel, and a bedroom, a second office and a full bath with a pebble-floor shower round out the level.

Because it walks out to grade, this floor gets real daylight and a direct connection to the yard, which is why it feels like part of the house and not a basement.

Outdoor living on every level

The covered rear deck extends the main floor outdoors, and below it a covered stone patio with its own fireplace gives the lower level an outdoor room that works in cooler weather. Beyond it, a paver patio with a fire-pit seating area looks over a pond with a fountain.

The landscaping carries the Craftsman character into the yard: boulder retaining walls, stone steps, planting beds and a small putting green tucked beside the patio.
